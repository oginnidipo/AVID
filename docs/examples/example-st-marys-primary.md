## Before

The room had been the school's library on paper since 2009. In practice it
stored broken furniture. The ceiling leaked over the north corner, one window
was boarded, and the single bookcase held 43 books, most of them donated
textbooks a decade out of date. Pupils were not allowed in, because there was
nothing to come in for.

## What we did

We repaired the ceiling and roof section above it, reglazed two windows,
rewired the room for four ceiling lights and installed a lockable steel door.
A carpenter in Mushin built the shelving, six reading tables and twenty-four
chairs from locally sourced hardwood — which cost less than importing and means
a broken chair can be repaired in an afternoon.

The collection was chosen with the school's two English teachers: 1,180 titles,
just over half by Nigerian and African authors, from Atinuke and Ifeoma
Onyefulu for the youngest classes through to Achebe, Adichie and Fágúnwà for
the senior pupils.

Mrs Adewale, who volunteered to take the library on, spent two days with us on
cataloguing, the borrowing system and running a reading period. The school
timetabled two reading periods per class per week before we finished the
building work.

## What changed

Three hundred and eighty of the school's 412 pupils now hold a borrowing card.
In the first term, 1,180 loans were recorded — very nearly one per book. The
head teacher moved the school's staff meetings into the library, which she said
was the first time the room had been worth meeting in.

We return in March for the twelve-month check.
